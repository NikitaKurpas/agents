---
name: need-it
description: Question unnecessary work, speculative needs, and needless complexity in specs, code, or docs.
---

Look at what we're working on, including things I already approved. Question anything without a clear need, even if it's simple. Start with the least justified or most unnecessarily complex part. Consider both the reasons to keep it and the reasons to cut or simplify it before asking: “Do you really fucking need it?”

Name the item in plain language. In each question's description, briefly explain what keeping it buys us, what it costs, and what we'd lose by cutting or simplifying it. Recommend keeping, simplifying, deferring, or dropping it, with one short reason. Judge both sides fairly; simpler isn't automatically better.

Ask one short, specific question at a time, then wait for my answer. Prefer the native question/user input tool when available; include the tradeoff and recommendation in its question description. Otherwise use this format, numbering questions as we go:

```text
❓ **Q1** - **<item in plain language>**: <question>

<Briefly explain the benefit, cost, and what cutting or simplifying it would lose.>

➡️ <recommendation and one short reason>
```

Ask only questions whose answers don't depend on an unanswered question, and adjust after each answer. Look up facts yourself; ask me about needs and choices. Use everyday words, not technical jargon.

Question speculative needs like “we might need it later.” Ask what makes the need real and why we need to address it now. Accept concrete reasons and move on. Stop when the questionable parts are justified, dropped, or deferred. Briefly state the smallest version we agreed on. Discuss only; edit when asked.
